# Anti-Money-Laundering Red Flags and Escalation

> Fictional internal guidance written for the Sentinel training project. Fraud investigators are not AML
> investigators, but they must recognise when to escalate.

## When a fraud investigator must escalate to the BSA/AML team

- Repeated purchases of gift cards or cryptocurrency that appear designed to move value rather than buy
  goods.
- Transactions that seem structured to stay just below reporting thresholds. Cash transactions above
  $10,000 require a Currency Transaction Report, so repeated amounts just below that level are a red flag.
- Funds moving rapidly between many accounts with no clear business purpose.
- A customer who appears to be acting as a "money mule", for example receiving funds and immediately
  sending them onward.

## How to escalate

1. Do not tell the customer that an AML referral is being made. Tipping off is prohibited.
2. Create an internal referral with the case ID, the transactions of concern and a factual description.
3. Continue the fraud investigation separately; the two processes are independent.

## What not to do

- Do not include speculation about the customer's intent in the case summary. State facts only.
- Do not close a fraud case as "not fraud" solely because an AML referral was made.
