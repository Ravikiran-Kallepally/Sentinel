# Regulation E - Electronic Fund Transfers (Educational Summary)

> This is a plain-language educational summary prepared for the Sentinel training project. It is not legal
> text and not legal advice. Always consult 12 CFR Part 1005 and the bank's compliance team.

## Scope

Regulation E implements the Electronic Fund Transfer Act. It protects consumers who use electronic fund
transfers (EFTs) such as debit card purchases, ATM withdrawals, ACH transfers and person-to-person payments
that move money out of a consumer asset account. Regulation E does not govern credit card accounts; credit
card disputes fall under Regulation Z (Truth in Lending).

## Consumer liability for unauthorized transfers

Consumer liability for an unauthorized electronic fund transfer depends on how quickly the consumer notifies
the financial institution after learning of the loss or theft of an access device such as a debit card:

- If the consumer notifies the institution within two business days after learning of the loss or theft,
  liability is limited to the lesser of $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers made before notice.
- If the consumer notifies the institution after two business days, liability can be up to $500.
- If the consumer fails to report an unauthorized transfer that appears on a periodic statement within
  60 days after the statement was sent, the consumer can face unlimited liability for transfers that occur
  after the 60-day period and before notice, if the institution can show the transfers would not have
  occurred had the consumer reported in time.

Negligence by the consumer, such as writing the PIN on the card, does not by itself increase liability
beyond these limits.

## Error resolution procedures

A consumer must give notice of an error within 60 days after the institution sent the periodic statement on
which the error first appeared. Notice may be oral or written.

After receiving notice, the institution must:

1. Investigate promptly and determine whether an error occurred within 10 business days.
2. If it cannot finish in 10 business days, it may take up to 45 calendar days, provided it gives the
   consumer provisional credit for the disputed amount within 10 business days of receiving notice.
3. For new accounts, point-of-sale debit transactions and foreign-initiated transfers, the timelines extend
   to 20 business days and 90 calendar days respectively.
4. Report the results to the consumer within 3 business days after completing the investigation.
5. If an error occurred, correct it within one business day after determining it.
6. If no error occurred, provide a written explanation and notify the consumer before reversing any
   provisional credit; the consumer may request the documents the institution relied on.

## Practical implications for investigators

- Record the date the customer first notified the bank; every deadline is measured from it.
- Provisional credit decisions must be made within 10 business days when an investigation will run longer.
- An investigation summary must state the evidence reviewed and the basis for the finding.
