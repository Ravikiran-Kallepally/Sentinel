# Card Dispute Handling - Standard Operating Procedure

> Fictional internal procedure written for the Sentinel training project.

## 1. Intake

- Disputes arrive by phone, secure message, branch or letter. Record the channel and the exact date and
  time the customer first notified the bank. This date starts every regulatory clock.
- Classify the dispute: unauthorized transaction, billing error (duplicate, wrong amount, missing credit),
  goods or services not received, or lost/stolen card.

## 2. Acknowledgment

- Send a written acknowledgment within 5 business days. This is stricter than the 30-day requirement under
  Regulation Z for credit cards.
- Tell the customer they do not need to pay the disputed amount while the dispute is open.

## 3. Provisional credit

- For unauthorized transactions under $500 on accounts older than 90 days, issue provisional credit within
  2 business days.
- For other disputes, issue provisional credit within 10 business days if the investigation is expected to
  take longer than 10 business days.

## 4. Investigation

- Follow the Card Fraud Investigation Playbook for unauthorized transactions.
- For billing errors, request the merchant's records through the card network and compare amounts, dates
  and receipts.
- Target resolution within 30 days; never exceed two complete billing cycles or 90 days.

## 5. Resolution and communication

- Notify the customer of the outcome in writing within 3 business days of the decision.
- If the dispute is denied, explain the reason in plain language and offer copies of the documents relied
  on.
- If a provisional credit will be reversed, give the customer at least 5 business days' notice.

## 6. Quality control

- 5% of closed disputes are re-reviewed every month by Quality Assurance.
- Disputes where an AI-generated summary was materially edited by the analyst are tagged so the AI team
  can review them in its evaluation process.
